# Confluence: classic price action

## Support and resistance
- A level matters more when it has several clean touches, a big reaction, or a higher timeframe behind it.
- **Flip:** broken support often becomes resistance on a retest, and broken resistance becomes support.
- A level is a **zone**, not a single tick. The agent gives zones as "≈ low to high".

## Trendlines and channels
- A trendline needs two touches to draw; a third touch makes it valid.
- A **parallel channel** is a trendline plus a parallel copy through the opposite swing. Useful parts:
  - the **edges** are reaction areas;
  - the **midline** (50%) often acts as a magnet and as support or resistance;
  - **slope** tells the trend: a falling channel is bearish, a rising channel is bullish.
- A **close** outside the channel on the working timeframe is a break. A wick outside that closes back inside is a possible sweep (see `confluence-ict.md`).
- A falling channel after a sharp drop can act as a bull flag. A break of its upper edge with follow-through is a common continuation-up signal.

## Candles at a level
- **Rejection wick (pin bar):** a long wick into a level and a close away from it.
- **Engulfing:** a candle body that fully covers the previous body, in the new direction.
- **Inside bar:** compression before expansion. Trade the break, not the bar.
- Candles only count **at a level**. In the middle of nowhere they are noise.

## Patterns
- Double top or bottom, and head and shoulders: reversal clues at the extremes of a range or channel.
- Flags and pennants: continuation after a strong move.
- Triangles: compression. The break direction matters more than the pattern.

## Round numbers (gold)
Gold often reacts at round numbers, for example every $10, and more strongly at $50 and $100 levels. Orders and stops cluster there, so expect sweeps around them.
