# Confluence: ICT / smart money concepts

These are supporting evidence for a playbook setup, never a trade on their own. All times are New York time.

## Liquidity
- **Buy-side liquidity** rests above swing highs and equal highs. That is where short sellers' stops and breakout buy orders sit.
- **Sell-side liquidity** rests below swing lows and equal lows.
- Obvious levels attract price, because orders are clustered there. Channel edges and round numbers count as obvious levels too.

## Liquidity sweep (stop hunt)
Price trades beyond a liquidity level, then **closes back inside** it. That is a clue the move was a grab for stops rather than a real breakout.
- A sweep of a channel edge, followed by a close back inside the channel, is strong confluence for an edge-reversal setup.
- Tight stops placed exactly at obvious levels are the ones that get swept. Put the stop beyond the sweep wick, or wait for the sweep to happen first.

## Market structure
- **Break of structure:** price breaks the last swing in the direction of the trend, so the trend continues.
- **Change of character / market structure shift:** the first break of the last swing *against* the trend, often right after a sweep. It is an early sign of reversal.
- A displacement candle (large, decisive) on the break makes the shift more convincing.

## Fair value gap
Three candles where the first candle's range and the third candle's range do not overlap:
- **bullish:** candle 3's low is above candle 1's high;
- **bearish:** candle 3's high is below candle 1's low.

Price often returns to partly fill the gap. An unfilled gap in the direction of the trade can be an entry area. A gap against the trade is a likely place for price to react.

## Order block
The last opposite-colour candle before a strong displacement move that breaks structure. When price returns to it, that is a possible entry area. Its strength is higher when it lines up with a channel edge or a Fibonacci level.

## Premium and discount
Within a dealing range (swing low to swing high):
- the **upper half** is "premium", a better place to look for sells;
- the **lower half** is "discount", a better place to look for buys.

The 0.5 Fibonacci level is the dividing line. ICT's "optimal trade entry" zone is roughly the 0.62–0.79 retracement.

## Session timing (killzones, approximate, New York time)
| Window | Time | Typical behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Asia | ~20:00–00:00 | Builds a range; its high and low become liquidity |
| London | ~02:00–05:00 | Often sweeps the Asia high or low, then sets the day's direction |
| New York AM | ~07:00–10:00 | US data at 08:30; continuation or reversal of London |

Different sources quote slightly different times. The agent records the session in every journal entry so the user's own data decides which windows work.
