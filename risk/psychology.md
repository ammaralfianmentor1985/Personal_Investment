# Psychology

## Market psychology: what the crowd does at levels
- **Stops cluster at obvious places:** beyond swing highs and lows, channel edges and round numbers. Price often sweeps them before reversing, especially at session opens. Tight stops at obvious levels are the first to go.
- **Fear is fast, greed is slow.** Panic drops are steep and quick. Rallies tend to grind. Capitulation candles (huge range, huge wick) often mark extremes.
- **Late breakouts trap latecomers.** People who missed the move buy the breakout late, become exit liquidity, and get stopped on the pullback.
- **News spikes:** spreads widen and fills slip. The first spike is often reversed.
- **Friday:** positions get closed before the weekend, and late-Friday moves can be erratic.

## Trader psychology: pre-trade checklist
Answer honestly. **Any bad answer means no trade.**
1. Is this a playbook setup, or am I bored or hoping?
2. Did I just lose, and do I want it back? (revenge)
3. Am I chasing a move that has already gone? (FOMO)
4. Is the risk within my normal dollar amount, and could I lose it right now calmly?
5. Do I know exactly where I am wrong (stop) and where I take profit?
6. Have I checked news and the spread?
7. Will I leave the stop where it is?

## Recommended habits (proposed; the user confirms them in `risk/rules.md`)
- Never move a stop further away from entry. Moving it closer, or to breakeven, by plan is fine.
- Never add to a losing position.
- Judge **execution** separately from **outcome**. A good trade can lose; a bad trade can win.
- Log every trade within the hour, while the feelings are still clear.
